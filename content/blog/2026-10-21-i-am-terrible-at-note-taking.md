This is ridiculous. It feels so weird to write by hand. I can’t believe I used to do this every day some years ago. And I used to rely on handwritten notes!

{{< blog-image src="images/blog/notetaking.jpg" alt="A handwritten draft of this post, with a pen resting across the page." >}}

My handwriting has always been terrible. I never had much manual dexterity or coordination. But this is a whole new ball game. I am now genuinely worried that in a few years I won’t even be able to sign my name on a piece of paper. With the likely adoption of speech-to-text software, even keyboard typing is at risk of atrophy.

How am I supposed to help my children with schoolwork? Maybe we don’t need to. Why should we bother avoiding the atrophy of deprecated, outdated tasks?

In middle school, I was likely better at mental arithmetic. Later, I either used calculators or moved on to symbolic calculations. During my math coursework, I was at my absolute prime when it came to computing limits, derivatives, integrals, partial differential equations, and proving simple theorems. Eventually, I mostly lost those raw skills. It’s what tends to happen when we don’t use them on a regular basis.

> “What you don’t use, you lose.”

Is that bad? Maybe not. Every task mentioned before is something that a machine can do vastly better. We might need to let some things go. If we are losing them, then we didn’t actually value them in the first place.

Unless the world ends. But is that really what we should be optimizing for?
